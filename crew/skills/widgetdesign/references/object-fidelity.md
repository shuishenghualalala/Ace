# Object Fidelity

Use this owner only when the request names or clearly implies a recognizable real object, mature
product, or established product category. The invariant is: **first recognizable as the requested thing**, then recognizable as Kimi's version of that thing. Object truth outranks house style.

## Recognizable object gate

Before visual styling, decide whether recognition depends on established physical or interface
anatomy. The gate is `yes` for named products, instruments, appliances, vehicles, devices, media
players, and other mature categories whose parts or controls set user expectations. It is `no` for
generic summaries, diagrams, comparisons, calculators, and abstract status surfaces.

When the gate is `yes`, research first and preserve the category skeleton. A title-swapped dashboard is a failure. Do not reduce an object to generic KPI cards, unrelated charts, or a standard app shell
with the object's name attached.

## Mode selection

Choose **Object-led mode** when the user names or clearly implies a named object, product, device,
material, or established visual style and that character is part of the desired result. An AMP music
player and an instant-camera or Polaroid-style widget enter Object-led mode. A generic category name
alone does not activate Object-led mode: `music player`, `weather widget`, or `budget tool` stays in
Default Kimi mode unless the user supplies a recognizable object/style reference.

In Object-led mode, research first and then design freely from the object's anatomy, silhouette,
material, controls, palette, and interaction. Do not force Construction Grid, interactive ASCII/Pixel,
Liquid Glass, Kimi palette, or Kimi material. Retain recognizable anatomy and affordances, P0
hierarchy, semantic recomposition, fit, accessibility, stable states, runtime security, and
copyright-safe abstraction. The result must not reuse the same default Kimi composition used for
unrelated objects.

When the user explicitly requests Kimi styling, preserve object anatomy first and selectively
re-enable the Kimi owners that strengthen rather than overwrite recognition. Record the mode before
visual styling; do not silently blend the two modes.

## Research and source evidence

Inspect sources in this order: **official product documentation -> official support or manufacturer documentation -> reliable secondary sources**. Prefer current manuals, product pages, support
articles, reputable teardowns, museum or standards references, and established specialist reviews.
Search snippets, memory, and AI summaries are discovery aids, not inspected evidence.

Record a compact evidence note before design:

- source title and URL or stable document identifier;
- source class and inspection date;
- directly observed anatomy, control, state, or behavior;
- uncertainty or disagreement between sources.

Never claim research without an inspected source. If no reliable source can be inspected, state the
gap and use only well-established category anatomy; do not invent product-specific details.

The supplementary Canvas/runtime source may inform host dimensions, tokens, and runtime behavior.
It is not Kimi visual authority and cannot override this package's brand owners.

## Anatomy and affordances

Create an **Indispensable anatomy inventory** before P0-P3. Include the parts whose absence would
make the result another category, plus the relationships that make those parts legible. For each
item record: visible form, user purpose, expected location/relationship, interaction or state, and
the inspected source that supports it.

Always preserve category affordances: controls must look and behave like the action users expect, state
must appear near the control or output it affects, and primary object relationships must survive all
tiers. Cosmetic details may simplify; identity-bearing parts and operational feedback may not.

Test recognition without the title, logo, brand name, or explanatory paragraph. If an informed user
cannot identify the category and primary use from silhouette, anatomy, and affordances, repair the
skeleton before adding Kimi treatment.

## Skeleton diversity

Derive layout from the object's own skeleton, not a universal dashboard template. A player may be
transport-led, a camera viewfinder-led, a mixer channel-led, a clock face-led, or a device
front-panel-led. Preserve meaningful asymmetry, control clusters, reading direction, and physical or
functional adjacency. Different object categories should produce visibly different skeletons even
when they share tokens, typography, Grid, Glass, or Pixel owners.

Responsive tiers recompose the same object. Compact keeps the smallest recognizable operational
silhouette; Regular restores primary category controls and state; Expanded may add evidence,
secondary controls, provenance, or explanatory detail. Never turn smaller tiers into generic cards.

## Copyright-safe abstraction

Do not copy a protected interface, trade dress, artwork, proprietary icon set, exact branded skin,
or pixel-for-pixel control arrangement. Preserve functional category conventions and indispensable
anatomy while abstracting proportions, spacing, typography, iconography, surface treatment, and
nonfunctional ornament into an original Kimi composition. Use product sources to understand what
the thing is and does, not as a tracing template.

When a branded detail is not required for category recognition or requested factual fidelity,
replace it with a neutral semantic equivalent. Never imply official endorsement or reproduce logos,
album art, photographs, or copyrighted media without an allowed supplied asset or clear permission.

## Omission rule

Kimi motifs are optional in Object-led mode. It does not require a Kimi-motif omission proof because the default signature does not
apply. In Default Kimi mode, Kimi motifs remain subject to the routed omission rules. Omit Grid, Glass, Pixel, texture,
depth, construction marks, or other Kimi decoration whenever it harms recognition, category
affordance, readable state, control use, fit, accessibility, or source-faithful anatomy. Record the
named reason; omission is a correct Kimi decision, not a brand failure.

Kimi likeness should come first from token roles, type hierarchy, restraint, semantic density, and
composition. Add at most the routed motifs that remain subordinate to object recognition and use.

## AMP player benchmark

Treat a requested AMP music player as a mature-product benchmark and research the named/current AMP
product before design. The minimum recognizable anatomy is:

- media identity: artwork or a copyright-safe placeholder, track title, artist, and source/context;
- transport cluster: previous, play/pause as the dominant control, and next in a coherent order;
- time model: elapsed time, duration, and a seek/progress control with current-value feedback;
- playback state: playing, paused, loading/buffering, unavailable/error, and disabled boundaries;
- volume control and current volume state;
- at least one player-specific secondary control: EQ/bands, queue, or device/output;
- other evidenced or requested audio affordances, such as shuffle, repeat, or quality state.

Volume and at least one player-specific secondary control belong to the family skeleton. Compact may omit volume and the player-specific secondary control, but must still read as a usable player rather than a metric card. Regular restores media identity, the primary time model, volume, and at least one researched secondary control. Expanded may add source, device, queue detail, or evidence while keeping transport dominant. Preserve transport adjacency and the progress/time relationship. Do not copy AMP's protected visual skin or exact interface; abstract the researched anatomy into an original Kimi player, and omit Kimi motifs that compete with playback.
